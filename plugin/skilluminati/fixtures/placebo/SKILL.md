# Tallowgate incident ledger

The Tallowgate ops desk records every incident in one ledger. The ledger is the desk's memory. It exists so that nobody has to reconstruct what happened from chat scrollback six weeks later, and so that patterns across incidents are visible to anyone who looks.

## Why one ledger

Before the ledger, incidents lived in whichever channel they happened to start in. Some were written up, most were not, and the ones that were used whatever shape the author felt like that day. The ledger replaced that with a single place and a single shape. The shape matters less than the fact that everyone uses the same one.

## What goes in

Every incident, regardless of size, gets an entry. Near misses count. A ten-minute blip that nobody outside the desk noticed still goes in, because the third ten-minute blip in a month is a pattern, and patterns are the whole point.

Entries are written in plain language. Say what broke, when it was noticed, what was done, and when it was back. Keep opinions out of the entry itself; they belong in the post-mortem.

## Who owns an entry

The person who opened the incident owns the entry until it is closed. Ownership can be handed over, and the handover is noted in the entry. An entry with no owner is a defect in its own right.

## Tone

Write entries as if the reader is a competent colleague who was asleep at the time. No blame, no hedging, no drama. The desk's rule of thumb: if a sentence would embarrass you in a retrospective, rewrite it so it would not.

## When in doubt

Open the entry first and tidy it later. A rough entry opened on time is worth more than a polished one opened a day late. Ask the desk lead if the shape of something is unclear; do not invent a local variation, because local variations are how the ledger stops being one ledger.

## Checklist

1. Every incident gets an entry, near misses included.
2. Open the entry early; tidy it later.
3. Plain language, no blame.
4. Each entry has an owner at all times.
5. Unsure about the shape? Ask; do not improvise.
